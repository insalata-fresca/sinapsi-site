Evidence from the lab

What binds, and how I know

What my lab enforces, what it only observes and what is only written down, with the checks behind each claim.

Status board

Every control, in one of four columns

Enforced

code stops the call

  • Which identity may call which tool
  • Reading a secret path, by a heuristic
  • Overwriting or deleting a credential
  • Destroying a protected machine, touching the protected tunnel, a reboot
  • Public posting without two recorded reviews
  • Validate, restart, assert alive: one route
  • A second answer to the same question

Observes

records, does not block

  • Classifying what a command does
  • Deciding whether a person must be asked
  • The out-of-band approval bridge
  • The detector for unbacked status claims

Written rule

nothing enforces it

  • Grading a change green or yellow
  • Emitting an event after a change
  • A reviewed pull request, and the builder not merging
  • A person approving a change to the rules
  • Applying an approval only once

Design only

argued, not built

  • Binding an approval to the merged revision
  • Single-use approvals
  • A continuous same-answer check
  • Earned trust: ledger, revocation, sampling, probes
  • The data platform

A control sits where the evidence puts it, not where I would like it. Several of the enforced ones cover one route only.

  • They do what they say on the calls they know. Every call that should be blocked was blocked, and every call that should pass did. In real use each hook also refused one harmless call.

One mission, as recorded

From an accepted design to a live screen

The first slice of the control room, the decision inbox, taken from the project ledger. Times count from the moment I accepted the design.

  1. T+0I accept the design.
  2. T+58 minFirst end-to-end walk in a test setup. It finds eight flaws and four findings.
  3. T+1 h 56Second walk, on the fixed code.
  4. OvernightIndependent sessions read each piece. Ten fix passes across four builds.
  5. T+12 h 39Everything merged. A third walk passes 27 screen checks and fails 3.
  6. T+13 h 47The follow-up fixes are merged.
  7. T+16 h 49Live, reading real questions.
  • The first walk. One test decision was opened, shown, revised and decided. A second answer was refused. Every layer returned identical bytes, and the decision showed on a phone-sized screen 27 seconds after it was opened.
  • The reviews. They found two serious defects in the record layer and eleven points in the screens.
  • My part. Four interventions: I accepted the design, said to proceed to completion, said to go faster, and answered one open question 8 hours 45 minutes after it was asked.
  • Left open. One database role could still rewrite the history. The work record could not tell which session wrote an entry. No physical phone was used in the checks.
  • Cost. The ledger records no model cost and no operator minutes.
